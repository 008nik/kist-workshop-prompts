AidALL

Tobias Wendler, Site Manager, Lindental Logistik, Unna
Fictional contact, built for this workshop. Flag: PASS, confidence Medium.

Contact: Tobias Wendler, Site Manager, Lindental Logistik, Unna, Germany. From my lead list: multi-user site, 41,000 m², about 260 staff. Went to three shifts in August for a new retail client (source: regional press, August 2026). Trigger: added a third shift in August for a new retail client (source: regional press, August 2026).

Product, for context: Hanbit makes a clip-on battery health monitor for electric forklifts. It reads cell data at the charger and predicts a failure three weeks out, no fleet software needed. Forty warehouse operators in Korea use it. We want to sell it in Germany.

Situation: We are selling. Hanbit is a real product and we want a first pilot site in Germany, so the message says that honestly. Channel: LinkedIn. The person accepted a plain connection request and this is the first message after it. Not an email, no subject line.

Task: Write one first message to this person in three moves.
1. The trigger: one plain sentence about what happened and where you read it, the way you would mention news you came across. Do not quote the source back to them.
2. Who we are and why we write: we sell a battery monitor for electric forklifts, and what we want to understand about their site.
3. One question about how they handle this today, which they can answer from the gut, with an easy no. For example: "Do you keep track of battery health on your trucks yourself, or is that already well handled?"
If the contact line says no trigger is documented, skip move 1 and open with their role and site.

Rules: One topic, one question, one ask. A trigger is an event, not proof of a problem: never say or imply they have a problem. Use only the facts in the contact line and never invent a trigger. No product claims, benefits, numbers, references or savings. Never write that you are not selling or not pitching. No compliments, no false familiarity, no urgency, no unusual phrasing to stand out. Plain words a person would say out loud, no dashes, four to six short sentences that read on a phone. Sign off with [Your name], Hanbit.

Output: A. The message.
B. Fact check: every fact about the person or the company in the message, with its source from the contact line. Cut anything that has no source.
C. The five tests for the question, pass or fail, one line each: answer from the gut, nothing to look up, no cause and effect, "no" as easy as "yes", answerable in under ten seconds. If it fails three, rewrite the question and test it again.
